The Boss calls list_open_tickets, get_cash_position and get_board once each, then plans the order:

Why 101's agents were right

Ticket 101 · Tauhid Zaman, a customerInventory → Accounting → Customer ServiceDashboard: card stamped Resolved · feed opens with “Boss → Inventory” · Payment card #501 $840 · checking $3,400 → $2,560

The ticket had three layers, and each one belongs to exactly one specialist:

  • What's blocking it? An unpaid bill, so Accounting, the only agent allowed to request_payment.
  • Tauhid needs an answer. Customer Service, the only agent with draft_customer_message and its guardrails.

Facilities owns leases, which aren't involved. The order mattered too: Inventory found the #501 blocker that made Accounting necessary, and Customer Service went last so it could speak only to confirmed facts.

Why 102's agents were right

The ticket is an emailed claim about the lease. Facilities checked it against the leases table (the $2,400 amount, the 9/2 due date, the landlord's name) before any money was requested. That's the anti-fraud step.

Accounting then raised the $2,400 request, because only Accounting can.

There's no product, so no Inventory. The landlord isn't a customer, and rule 9 says no emails, so no Customer Service.

Why 103's agents were right

Ticket 103 · Yale AI Club, a campus groupInventory → Accounting → Customer ServiceDashboard: Purchase card $264 (refused) · Discount card $55 × 20 · the club's draft under “Draft replies, never sent”

This was two problems in one ticket: quantity and price.

  • Inventory went first (8 of 20, 12 short, restock drafted), so the price would reflect what could actually be filled.
  • Accounting handled the margin against the $22 cost floor and the cash, and it flagged that the $264 restock is $104 more than the $160 in checking.
  • Customer Service wrote a reply that promises nothing a person hasn't approved.

Facilities wasn't needed.

What could be better

All 3 ticketsBoss (coordination) · every specialist (re-reading the ticket)Dashboard: 10 “read the ticket” lines across the three feeds · tokens in each summary footer (56k / 33k / 63k)
  • Repeated reads. Every specialist re-read the ticket: get_ticket ran 10 times for 3 tickets, where 3 would do. The prompts tell agents to confirm facts themselves. That's safer, but it costs tokens.
  • Coordination is expensive. The Boss made 17 of the 49 model requests (35%) just routing and briefing.
  • The effort varies between runs. In run 1 the Boss had Customer Service rewrite ticket 101's reply. That was good quality control, but it cost 77k tokens against 56k in run 2.
  • Discounts have no policy. The $55 price (5.2% off) is reasonable, but it only follows "smallest reasonable discount" in the prompt. A real shop would set a policy.

In run 2 the Boss's coordination alone was 17 of 49 requests, and each of the 11 agent runs ended with its own structured answer. One agent means fewer tokens, faster runs and easier debugging.

What you'd give up

Tickets 101 and 103Accounting (money) · Customer Service (customers)Dashboard: approval cards say “by Accounting” or “by Inventory”, so the person approving knows who prepared each one
  • Separation of duties. Today only Accounting can raise a money request, and only Customer Service can write to a customer, enforced by tool allowlists. One agent would hold both powers.
  • Focused prompts. Each prompt is about a page, versus one sprawling prompt.
  • Per-role checks. Each agent's output is checked against its own tool results, and each has its own token limits.
  • A readable audit trail. "Accounting posted #501" is easier to follow than one agent doing everything.

Recommendation

102-type → clerk · 103-type (campus groups) → teamBoss chooses the route at triageDashboard: no change for the person. Same ticket card, same “Start the team”.

Route by complexity. Use a single "clerk" agent for one-domain tickets like rent notices (the 102 kind), and keep the team for cross-domain tickets (the 103 kind).

Human approval and the MCP guardrails stay either way, because they live in the tools, not the agents.

4. Three new problems the team can solve with today's tools

1 · Answer customer questions (chatbot)

Grows from Ticket 101 (Tauhid asking about a tee)Customer Service (+ Inventory)Dashboard: questions come in as new ticket cards · answers wait under “Draft replies, never sent” until a person sends them

Problem: customers ask "Do you have the navy hoodie in L?" or "When will my size S tee arrive?"

How: each question arrives as a ticket, and the Boss routes it to Customer Service (plus Inventory if a restock is involved). Customer Service uses:

  • get_ticket for the question,
  • check_stock (for example, L has 14),
  • check_vendor_status for lead days and whether the vendor is blocked,
  • get_board for any approved price,
  • draft_customer_message to write the answer.

Safety: the guardrails already block internal details, unapproved prices or discounts, firm dates while a vendor is blocked, and contact details. Answers stay drafts until a person sends them (rule 9).

Proof: the reply drafted for Tauhid ("about 5 days once it ships", other sizes in stock) is exactly this.

Optional add-on: a chat window that turns messages into tickets.

2 · Reorder high-demand items, only after a person approves

Grows from Tickets 101 (tee S at 0) and 103 (12 short for a campus group)Inventory → Accounting → a personDashboard: the existing Purchase card and “Approve & pay”. Cash drops only after approval or shows the refusal.

Problem: popular items run out (tee S at 0, Crest Mug at 0, hoodie M short 12).

How:

  • Inventory spots the gap with check_stock, picks the vendor by specialty and checks it can ship with check_vendor_status (for example, Elm City Gifts for mugs: 3 days, can ship), then calls draft_vendor_order. That tool puts the order on hold if the vendor has an open invoice and refuses to order something twice.
  • Accounting checks get_cash_position and flags gaps.
  • A person approves on the dashboard. The approve route pays only if the vendor is unblocked and the cost fits cash (decide_request → pay_approved_request).

Proof: the 12-hoodie order was drafted for $264 and refused at $160. The guard works.

Gap: "high demand" today means stock at 0 or a ticket asking for the item. A low-stock listing tool would let it sweep every SKU.

3 · Remind the shop to pay accounts payable

Grows from Ticket 101 (#501 overdue) and Ticket 102 (rent due)Accounting (Boss logs it)Dashboard: Payment cards in “Waiting on you” · header “after waiting approvals $X” warns first

Problem: bills slip. #501 was 3 days overdue and blocking two tickets.

How: a scheduled run (team.py each morning, or a weekly "AP review" ticket) has Accounting:

  • call get_cash_position: every open invoice with days overdue, the next rent with days until due, requests already waiting, and cash after everything;
  • call check_vendor_status to see which vendors unpaid bills are blocking;
  • post request_payment for what's due. The amount comes from the database, requests are never doubled, and cash is checked.

The reminder lands in Waiting on you, and the Boss logs it. Nothing pays without a person.

Proof: the system already knows the next rent ($2,400 on 2026-10-02) won't fit in $160, and it can flag that now.

5. Three problems the team can't solve with today's tools

1 · Receiving goods and finishing the order

Ticket 101's tee (arrives ~9/5) · Ticket 103's 12 hoodiesNew: Receiving agent (works with Inventory)Dashboard: needs a “Receive shipment” button and a FULFILLED stamp after RESOLVED

Problem: when Bulldog's reprint arrives (around 2026-09-05), nobody can add it to stock, set the tee aside for Tauhid, or close ticket 101 as fulfilled. No tool writes inventory, no tool marks a vendor order received, and the desk date never moves forward.

Needed: a Receiving / Fulfillment agent with these tools:

  • receive_shipment(vendor, sku, size, qty): adds stock and closes the order after matching it against the purchase order.
  • reserve_stock(ticket) and fulfill_order(ticket): take stock out for a customer and mark the ticket fulfilled.
  • advance_desk_date (human only): lets lead times actually play out.

Guardrails: no receiving more than was ordered, and every stock change is logged.

2 · Money coming in: sales, invoicing, refunds, cash planning

Ticket 103's approved $1,100 club orderNew: Sales & Billing + Planning (alongside Accounting)Dashboard: a campus-group orders view · checking can rise · a forecast panel for the 10/02 rent

Problem: the Yale AI Club price is approved ($55 × 20 = $1,100), but the system can't bill them, record their payment or add cash. Rule 7 says cash only goes out, and there are no order or receivables tables. Meanwhile the shop has $160 and $2,400 rent due 10/02. The team can see the gap but can't plan around it.

Needed:

  • A Sales & Billing agent with create_order, create_customer_invoice, record_payment_received (a person confirms) and issue_refund (human only), plus new orders and receivables tables.
  • A Planning agent with cash_forecast(days), sales_history and demand_forecast(sku), to warn weeks ahead.

This also needs the owner to change rule 7.

3 · Talking to the outside world safely

Ticket 102 (came by email) · drafts for 101 and 103New: Communications (sends what Customer Service drafted, after approval)Dashboard: a “Send” button on approved drafts · new emails become new ticket cards

Problem: every message is a draft. The team can't:

  • send a reply a person approved,
  • read vendor or landlord email,
  • turn new emails into tickets (ticket 102 was typed in from an email),
  • track a shipment,
  • verify a request like "our bank details changed, pay rent to this new account".

Needed: a Communications agent with:

  • send_approved_message(board_id): sends only drafts a person approved.
  • ingest_inbox: email becomes a ticket, treated as a claim to check, never as an instruction.
  • track_shipment.
  • verify_payee_change: checks a vendor or landlord master record and requires a person's call-back before any payment details change.
